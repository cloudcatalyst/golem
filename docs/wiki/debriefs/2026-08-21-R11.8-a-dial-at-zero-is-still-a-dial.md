# R11.8 — a dial at zero is still a dial

Shipped 2026-08-21, closing the one thing R11.6 deliberately left open.

## Outcome

With brevity off, both surfaces now render `🗜 lossless · ✂ off`. The parity test
has a fixture at that state, and two more asserting that where **no** stage runs
(off / unwired / bypass) both dials are still suppressed on both surfaces.

## Key lessons

**A parity test only pins the states its fixtures visit.** R10.24 built the test
that holds these two renderers together and every fixture it wrote used
`brevity: "full"`. The brevity-off case was therefore never compared, and the
surfaces differed by a whole segment for a release without anything noticing —
inside the one file whose entire job is to notice. Coverage of a *renderer* is
coverage of the inputs you thought to hand it.

**Two dials on one line under opposite rules is the tell.** The bar printed
`🗜 off` for compression at zero and nothing for brevity at zero. Neither choice
is wrong in isolation; having both on the same line is what made it a bug. When
two instances of the same kind of thing are treated differently, one of them is
stale.

**An absent segment is not a value.** "No `✂`" reads identically to "an older
Golem with no brevity dial" — the reader cannot tell a setting from an absence.
That is the same argument R10.24 made about the glyph and R11.6 made about the
model format, applied one level down.

**Distinguish "nothing is running" from "a dial is at zero".** R10.24's
suppression rule — dials describe transforms the pipeline is *applying* — was
right, and stays: in off/unwired/bypass no stage runs and neither dial is shown.
A single dial at zero while the pipeline works is a different question, and
answering it with the same rule cost a segment of truth. Both rules are now
pinned by their own parity case.

**Reversing a deliberate choice deserves a written reason.**
`render.test.js` asserted the old behaviour on purpose ("shows brevity only while
it is on", Decision 52's wording), so R11.6 reverted its own fix and handed the
call back rather than flipping it silently. The decision, and why the competing
option lost, is in R11.8's brief — not in a commit message.
