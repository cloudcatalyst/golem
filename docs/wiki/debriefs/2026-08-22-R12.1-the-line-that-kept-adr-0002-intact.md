# R12.1 — ADR-0006, and the line that kept ADR-0002 intact

The threat-model ADR that R6.3 had been blocked on since 2026-07-30. Accepted
2026-08-21 as spec **Decision 59**, in two revisions, and corrected once more the
next day. See [[Plugin Seams]] for the ADR-0005 precedent it deliberately copies.

## Outcome

`ADR-0006`, ACCEPTED, plus Decision 59's nine lettered clauses. R6.3 unblocked
after 52 days. The batch it gates is R12.2–R12.11 — of which R12.2, R12.6 and
R12.7 shipped within the day, and five tasks were paused before they started.

## Key lessons

**1. The load-bearing move was narrowing the capability until the existing
invariant survived.** The obvious way to build remote approval is to let a phone
answer any prompt, which forces an amendment to ADR-0002's invariant 5 — *"there
is no level whose matrix auto-allows destructive/outward"*. Instead the ADR
confines remote approval to `read`, `write` and `unknown`, and **never**
`destructive` or `outward`, with no setting to change it. ADR-0002 then needs no
amendment at all: `allow` is still never emitted for those classes, from any
device, at any level. A stolen phone cannot push, publish, deploy, `ssh` or
delete.

That reframing also produced the honest product sentence: *a phone keeps the agent
moving; it does not let you ship from the beach.* Writing the limitation as a
feature description rather than a caveat is what made the trade obvious enough to
accept.

**2. Ask which invariant a feature would break before designing the feature.** The
first draft went looking for mitigations. The version that got accepted went
looking for the *narrowest capability that broke nothing*, and found it. Most of
the threat-model table's "Yes, structurally" rows fell out of that one decision
rather than being argued for individually.

**3. Two of the three hard parts were already built, and the ADR's job was to
notice.** `runNotificationHook` → `.golem/state/session.json` → `/api/state` on
`127.0.0.1` already existed, with `session-state.ts`'s own header calling a
network surface "21b's later, guarded step". `src/proxy/loopback-cert.ts` (R9.12)
already mints X.509 CA and leaf certificates over `node:crypto` with
`nameConstraints` and `pathlen:0` — so mTLS needed no dependency and the five-dep
runtime pin held. An ADR that had not read the tree would have specified both from
scratch.

**4. A recorded non-negotiable is worth reversing out loud.** R6.3's brief said
"self-hosted relay only — no Golem-operated service in the path". The user
reversed it mid-review: the app must work over the internet, behind an account.
Revision 2 records that **as a reversal**, names the clause it supersedes and the
clauses that still stand, and points at Decision 20c having left relay
architecture explicitly open. The alternative — quietly editing the design so the
contradiction disappears — would have destroyed the only record that the
constraint was ever considered.

**5. The reversal was made safe by three constraints nobody asked for.** The
user's instruction was "internet, behind an account". What made it acceptable was
adding: the relay is blind (terminates no TLS, holds no key); **enrolment is
local-only, so a fully compromised account cannot pair a device**; and 2FA is
mandatory. That middle one is the whole security argument — it is why account
takeover costs DoS and metadata rather than code execution. Constraints invented
during review, then ratified explicitly, rather than assumed.

**6. An ADR should be willing to reach "no".** Alternative 1 is *do not build
it*, kept genuinely open, and the ratification list says declining the authorize
tier cancels R12.3 alone and leaves the rest useful. That structure paid off
within 24 hours: when R12.7 found Anthropic shipping capability 2 first-party,
five tasks could be paused against Decision 59(i) without touching the ADR's
status, because the threat model is transport-independent and was written that
way.

**7. Three "No, no mitigation" rows are the honest core.** A paired unlocked
device is RCE authority; a remote human approves with worse context than a local
one; the relay learns metadata. Stated *before* the design, per ADR-0005. And
Decision 59(g) had to be corrected the next day — it claimed capability 3 "does
not exist", which R12.7 disproved. The fix was to keep the outcome and replace the
reason: Golem **declines** to let a phone start a turn, because authoring a turn
is a larger authority than answering one. **An ADR that says "impossible" ages
badly; one that says "declined, and here is why" does not.**
