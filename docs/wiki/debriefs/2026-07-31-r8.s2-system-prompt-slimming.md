# R8.S2 — system-prompt slimming, declined

**Verdict: declined, not deferred. No code shipped.** The spike existed to produce a
publishable sentence and stop the idea recurring, and §100's lesson is that the
arithmetic *is* the deliverable.

## The measurement

Third independent capture of the system prompt on this project's traffic — §95: 3,350,
§100: 3,365, here **3,347** tokens. Remarkably stable, and **0.92% of a 362,283-token
request**.

Cost basis from the same day's real traffic (`golem bench cost`, the 20 samples R8.8
now attributes): `in 40 / write 12,359 / read 9,627,462 / out 4,738` over 20 requests →
**~48,912 full-price-input-token equivalents per request**, ~96% of it cache reads.

## What a trim would collect

Removed bytes are prefix bytes, so they are saved at the 0.1× cache-read rate:

| trim | tokens | equivalents/request | share of input cost | per month here |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| 10% | 335 | 33.5 | 0.068% | $7.54 |
| 30% | 1,004 | 100.4 | 0.205% | $22.59 |
| 50% | 1,674 | 167.4 | 0.342% | $37.66 |

## What it risks

A rewrite whose bytes move between turns busts the tools+system prefix on **every**
request: 362k reprices from 0.1× to 1×, **+326,055 equivalents ≈ 6.7× a normal
request**, i.e. **3,247× the per-request saving of a 30% trim**. Golem has already
measured that shape once — §103, Headroom on caching traffic, 8.7×–11.3× worse than
doing nothing.

Byte-stability *is* achievable: Decision 52's marker-fenced brevity append proves it.
So the risk is manageable rather than inevitable. It is the asymmetry that decides.

## Why it is a no even at $22/month

1. **The bytes are not Golem's.** `system` is where the client puts the instructions
   that make its harness work. Trimming them is a fidelity change, and the hard rule is
   byte-faithful passthrough at level ≤1 — so this could only ever be level 2/3
   behaviour, where Decision 23 already says the input axis is ~0% on caching traffic.
2. **Nothing in it is provably inert.** The one transform this project ever proved
   invisible — dropping `$schema` (§100) — was worth 72 tokens (0.05%) and was still
   rejected for mutating a cached prefix. There is no analogous ignorable region of a
   system prompt; every candidate cut is a behaviour bet.
3. **0.2% is not worth a behaviour bet on the highest-consequence bytes in the
   request.**

## The pattern

Fourth consecutive input-side idea to die on the same two questions — **whose bytes are
they, and how big is the prize actually**: §100 (tool schemas), §103 (semantic
compression on caching traffic), §107 (caveman-shrink), §108 (this).

Reopen only with a provably inert region *and* a byte-stability proof, and measure
against the table above first.
