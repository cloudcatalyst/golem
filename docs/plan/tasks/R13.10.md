## Why this exists now, unbuilt

The user asked to *plan* for internet reach while keeping v1 on the LAN. Filing
it now does two useful things and costs nothing: it stops phase 2 arriving by
accident (invariant 9), and it tells R13.4/R13.5 what they must not preclude.

The single most important consequence is already recorded in ADR-0007 §7c and
worth repeating here: **v1's device certificate is phase 2's transport
security.** That is why mTLS was revived on the LAN rather than something
smaller — so this task is a change of *reach*, not of trust model.

## What phase 2 must inherit rather than re-decide

Decision 59 accepted these in detail. They are constraints, not open questions:

- **The relay is blind, structurally.** The mTLS session terminates on the
  developer's machine and nowhere else, so the relay copies ciphertext between
  two sockets: terminates no TLS, holds no key, has no certificate from any
  user's CA, and has no code path that emits a decision. It unavoidably learns
  account, timing, device count and traffic volume — stated, not mitigated.
- **Enrolment stays local-only, forever** (invariant 8). No relay-mediated
  pairing and no message type for one, so a fully compromised account yields a
  pipe with nothing authorized at the far end: account takeover costs DoS and
  metadata, not code execution.
- **The account buys rendezvous and nothing else**; 2FA mandatory; no other
  Golem capability requires an account.
- **Silence denies** (invariant 3). A relay outage is silence. It never degrades
  to direct exposure and never queues a decision to apply later.
- **Self-hosting stays tested**, which is the standing condition on which a
  hosted relay is acceptable at all (Decision 59(h)) — if it lapses, golem.run
  becomes load-bearing and the local-first positioning breaks.

## What this task decides that Decision 59 did not

- Whether the endpoint is a Golem-operated service, a self-hosted one, or simply
  a network the user already runs (Tailscale, WireGuard). **Price the third
  option honestly before building the first** — for a single developer's devices
  it may be the whole answer, and it carries no uptime, abuse or PII duty.
- What the app shows about *where* it is connected, so a user always knows
  whether they are on the LAN or through a relay.
- The Decision 59(h) obligations that come with operating anything: uptime, abuse
  response, incident handling, and the identity/PII ADR that a hosted account
  system needs before it stores data about a person.

## Out of scope

Anything on the LAN path (R13.3–R13.9). Standing up infrastructure — that is an
outward, credentialed act and needs its own `owner: user` task, as R12.9 was.
Reviving R12.8/R12.9's briefs wholesale. Multi-user or team access.

## Gate detail

Before any code: a short section in verification-notes pricing the three endpoint
options against a single developer's actual need, and a recommendation. Then, if
a relay is built: a test that the relay cannot decrypt (assert on the wire, not
in prose), an outage test proving silence denies, a documented and *exercised*
self-hosting path, and the ADR revision recording which option won and why.
